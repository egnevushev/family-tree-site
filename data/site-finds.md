# Finds

Text before the first «## » heading does not go to the site. A find is «## <id>: <title>», then the lines «Years:», «Where:» (places, comma-separated), «People:» (ids, comma-separated, the main person first), «Document:» (optional: a C… whose source has an image; its scan stands beside the find), «In short:» (one line for the folded find) and, if needed, «Rests on:» (the weak link, with references). After an empty line come the paragraphs of the story, with references [C…], [R…], [X…], [S:…]. Write for the whole family, with full names: who each person is to the reader, the site counts itself. Nothing about children or the private lives of the living. Finds go in order of time. The Russian version is site-finds.ru.md, with the same ids, people, years and document.

## census-1858: A boy of twelve in the census of 1858
Years: 1858
Where: Peski
People: ivan-1858, matvey
Document: C040
In short: The census of the village of Peski lists a twelve-year-old Ivan, son of Pyotr Ilyin Sokolov — perhaps the very «Ivan Sokolov» of the family chart.
Rests on: That this Ivan was Matvey’s father is a conclusion, not a record: the same village, the same surname, a fitting age, and no other Ivan in the village [C041]. The family chart names the father only as «Ivan Sokolov» [R020].

The family chart names the father of Matvey Ivanovich Sokolov in two words: «Ivan Sokolov» [R020]. Who he was, the chart does not say.

An answer worth checking turned up in the census of 1858 for the village of Peski. In the household of Pyotr Ilyin Sokolov it lists «his son Ivan Petrov», twelve years old [C040]. Matvey, a peasant of the same village [C032], was born about 1868 [C045], when this Ivan would have been about twenty-two.

Until a record of Matvey’s birth is found, the tree draws this link dashed, as a possibility [C041].

## matvey-age: A year of birth read in a death record
Years: 1868–1925
Where: Peski
People: matvey, ivan
Document: C031
In short: No record of Matvey Sokolov’s birth has been found, yet the clerk who wrote down his death in 1925 also wrote down his age: fifty-seven.
Rests on: The year is counted from the age, not read in a birth record [C045].

Matvey Ivanovich Sokolov, a peasant of the village of Peski and a carpenter, died on 2 November 1925 [C032][C030]. The death record gives his age as fifty-seven [C031].

So Matvey was born about 1868 [C045]. It is the only date of his birth there is so far, and it is how the tree places him.

His trade went on in the family: his son Ivan Matveevich, born in 1898, was a carpenter like his father [R019][C019][C023].

## father-grigory: A priest among the New Martyrs
Years: 1872–1937
Where: Uglich
People: grigory, alexey
In short: Grigory Vasilyevich Vershinin served as a priest in Uglich for almost forty years; in 1937 he was arrested and shot, and in 2000 he was glorified among the New Martyrs.
Rests on: That Alexey Grigoryevich Vershinin was his son is known from the hagiography alone [R018], and Alexey is linked to his own son Sergey only in someone else’s tree [R017].

Grigory Vasilyevich Vershinin was born in 1872, and in 1898 he was ordained priest to the Intercession church of Uglich [C033][C034].

On 2 October 1937 he was arrested in Uglich [C035]. On 15 November an NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and on 20 November the sentence was carried out [C036][C037]. He was rehabilitated in 1989 [C038], and in 2000, in this demo, he was glorified among the New Martyrs [C039].

His hagiography mentions a son, Alexey [R018]. Alexey’s son, Sergey Alekseevich Vershinin, taught history in the same town of Uglich [R017][C016].

## volkhov: Born in 1898 — or in 1897?
Years: 1898–1942
Where: Kesova Gora, Volkhov Front
People: ivan, matvey
Document: C019
In short: The parish register and the Book of Remembrance disagree by a year about the birth of Ivan Matveevich Sokolov, who was killed on the Volkhov Front.
Rests on: The disagreement is open [X01]: the register is a document of the time, the Book of Remembrance a later publication.

Ivan Matveevich Sokolov was born on 14 January 1898 and baptised the next day in St Elijah church of the village of Kesova Gora. The parish register names his father, the carpenter Matvey Ivanov Sokolov [C019][C020][R019].

In August 1941 he was called up by the Kashin district military office as a private, and in 1942 he was killed on the Volkhov Front [C022][C021]. The Book of Remembrance gives his year of birth as 1897 [X01].

The register was written at his baptism, so the site follows it. The question stays open until a military registration card or an entry in a household book is found [X01].

## kalinin-1958: The carpenter’s son and the buoy keeper’s daughter
Years: 1931–1958
Where: Kashin, Kalinin
People: nikolay, zinaida, petr
Document: C011
In short: In 1958 in Kalinin, Nikolay Sokolov, born in Kashin and the son of a carpenter killed in the war, married Zinaida Belova, the daughter of a buoy keeper on the Volga.
Rests on: The fathers’ trades are known from the family’s account [C023][C027]; the marriage and Nikolay’s birth, from certificates [C011][C006].

Nikolay Ivanovich Sokolov was born on 15 February 1931 in the town of Kashin [C006][C007]. He was still a boy when his father, Ivan Matveevich, was killed on the Volkhov Front [R011][C021].

Zinaida Petrovna Belova came from the Volga: her father, Pyotr Ilyich Belov, kept the buoys on the river near Uglich [R016][C027].

On 14 June 1958 they married in Kalinin [C011][R007]. Nikolay worked there as a mechanical engineer at the railway car works [C009] and lived in Kalinin, today Tver, until his death in 2004 [C010][C008].
